# Game mechanics

Every campaign is a persistent political system. A turn validates one action, pays its costs, applies direct effects, updates memories and coalitions, resolves a legal opponent action, advances political/economic dynamics, matures delayed consequences, checks conditional events, evaluates endings, records history, and commits an integrity-checked save.

## Presidential campaign

The fictional 2028 campaign covers 50 states and Washington, D.C. with 538 electors and a 270-vote threshold. State support begins from fictional conditions and moves with state investment, national sentiment, momentum, opponent strategy, turnout, and seeded volatility. Rallies are cheaper and mobilizing; advertising is stronger but expensive; fundraising replenishes cash at a momentum cost; debate preparation and endorsements trade resources for national or faction effects. Election night resolves every state once and produces a complete map.

## The Republic

Political survival depends on the Economic Council, Security Directorate, Provincial Governors, Civic Forum, cabinet, assembly, courts, public sentiment, legitimacy, corruption, and a five-variable economy. Coalition management can stabilize the short term while weakening resources. Reform creates delayed employment benefits and elite tension. A late managed transition can produce a constitutional ending; low legitimacy or coalition support produces collapse.

## The Crown

The player balances the royal household, great houses, Charter League, Temple Concord, treasury, institutions, and an aging cast. Preparing the heir builds dynastic continuity; diplomatic marriage is an abstract consensual alliance; charter reform can create a constitutional crown. Characters age every four turns. Stable dynasty, constitutional transition, and succession collapse are mechanically distinct endings.

## The Transition

This mode is a fictional, high-level contest of legitimacy. It models public confidence, provincial support, civil-service continuity, courts, dialogue, recognition, and post-transition capacity. It provides no operational guidance for violence, weapons, infrastructure compromise, or real-world illegal conduct. A credible charter and resilient institutions are necessary; momentum alone cannot produce durable government.

## Difficulty, recovery, and replay

Difficulty changes starting resources and opponent decision strength. It does not bypass resource rules. Poor turns are recoverable through resource-building, coalition repair, institutional investment, and long-horizon economic choices. Seeds reproduce scenario randomness. Distinct leaders, seeds, modes, opponent policies, and event paths support replay without energy timers or paid access.
